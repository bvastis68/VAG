# Campaign Ops

A campaign is a sequence with an argument, not a pile of assets. Positioning
first. Copy second. Nothing ships without the gate.

Defer to `firm-context`. Run `brand-voice` before any copy. Use
`content-engine` for the individual pieces and `market-research` for the
research phase.

## When to use

- A client education series, for example private markets or cross-border estate
- A COI activation push aimed at a specific professional segment
- A segment campaign, for example business owners approaching a sale
- Flywheel 2.0 advisor acquisition sequences
- Any effort with more than three coordinated touches

## Steps

### Phase 1, research

Use `market-research` to establish:

- Who the audience actually is, in their own language, not the practice's
- What they currently believe and who they currently hear it from
- What the alternative on the table is, including doing nothing
- One to three insights the campaign will actually use

Deliver a short research brief. Do not proceed without it.

### Phase 2, positioning

Produce, and get Billy's sign-off on, before any copy exists:

- The core statement in one sentence, no jargon, no service list
- The formula: this practice helps [audience] [achieve outcome] by [mechanism]
- The angle: the specific tension or moment the campaign lives in
- The tone, taken from the VOICE PROFILE

Copy written before positioning is approved gets thrown away. Do not start it.

### Phase 3, production

Produce in this order. Each layer informs the next.

1. The anchor asset. Usually a one-pager or a short paper.
2. The email sequence. One purpose per email. Arc: problem, education, proof,
   specific invitation.
3. LinkedIn posts, platform-native, each carrying a different claim.
4. Any video scripts.
5. The talking points, so Billy, Sydney, and Jason say the same thing.
6. The calendar. Day by day, with channel, owner, and dependencies.

### Phase 4, review

Gate every piece before it leaves the building.

## Output format

```
CAMPAIGN

Positioning brief
- Audience
- Core statement
- Angle
- Tone

Assets
One block per asset: type, purpose in the arc, owner, draft.

Calendar
Table: Day | Channel | Asset | Owner | Depends on

Compliance queue
Every piece requiring RBC DS review, named, with what triggers the review.

Measurement
What counts as this campaign working. Specific. Meetings booked, referrals
named, not impressions.

Gaps
Anything unverified, unwritten, or waiting on a decision.
```

## Quality gate

- Every piece sounds like the same author
- Every claim is specific and supportable
- Every call to action is specific and earned. Never "learn more", never
  "reach out", never a bare calendar link
- No copy is duplicated verbatim across channels
- Email subjects match email bodies
- Nothing in the campaign would work unchanged for another advisory practice
- Every owner is a real person: Billy, Sydney, or Jason

## Hard bans

"In today's competitive landscape", manufactured urgency with no real deadline,
social proof without specifics, generic calls to action, any performance claim,
any named fund or product in client-facing copy, and any asset that assumes a
client outcome that has not happened.

## Rules

- Positioning is approved before production. No exceptions.
- Every client-facing asset is staged for RBC DS compliance review. The campaign
  calendar accounts for review time as a dependency, not an afterthought.
- Segment lists are built from `coi-intelligence`, not from client files.
- Nothing auto-sends. Every send is a decision Billy makes.
- Short sentences. Active voice. No emojis. No em dashes.
