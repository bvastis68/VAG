# Competitive Positioning

Three questions, answered in order: who does this practice actually compete
with, how does it compete, and which part of the field is genuinely open.
Anything that does not move toward those three gets cut.

Defer to `firm-context` for the practice's stated positioning. Use
`market-research` for the evidence pass underneath this.

## When to use

- A prospect is comparing this practice against an incumbent or another firm
- Deciding where to invest in differentiation
- Flywheel 2.0: understanding what an advisor would be leaving and joining
- Building the argument in an investment proposal or a COI conversation

## Establish first

Before any comparison, state the practice's intended position. Without it there
is no axis to measure against.

- The tension: the two axes that define the target space. For this practice the
  usual pair is depth of planning against genuine access.
- The differentiator: cross-border capability, family-office service model,
  real private markets allocation, tax-aware structuring.
- The target quadrant: where the practice intends to sit.

The central empirical question is whether that quadrant is actually open.

## Steps

1. Define the category. Be honest about it. A prospect comparing this practice
   to a discount platform is in a different category than one comparing it to
   another full-service team.
2. Tier the competitors.
   - Direct: other full-service advisory teams serving the same HNW and UHNW
     cross-border clients
   - Adjacent: private banks, family offices, discretionary platforms, discount
     brokerages, robo platforms
   - Aspirational: the multi-family offices the practice is growing toward
3. Build the comparison matrix. Competitors as rows grouped by tier. Dimensions
   as columns. Include this practice as a row with an honest self-assessment.
   The honest row is the entire point.
4. Map the field. Plot every competitor and the practice on the tension axes.
   Show whether the target quadrant is crowded or open.
5. Name the white space, and say what it would take to hold it.
6. Recommend. Two or three moves, no more.

## Default dimensions

Adjust with Billy, but state them every time.

- Cross-border CA and US capability, in practice not in brochure
- Planning depth: retirement, estate, tax, business succession
- Alternatives access and actual allocation, not availability
- Tax-aware implementation: asset location, loss harvesting, deferral
- Service model and client-to-advisor ratio
- Fee model and transparency
- Reporting quality and cadence
- Continuity and bench depth
- Brand and referral gravity

## Output format

```
COMPETITIVE POSITION
Date: [today] | Confidence: High / Medium / Low

Executive summary
Three to five decision-first takeaways. Where the practice is strong, where it
is exposed, who is sitting in its target space, the top two or three moves.

Category and field map
The category, the axes, and where everyone sits including this practice.

Tiers
Direct, adjacent, aspirational. One short paragraph each.

Comparison matrix
Competitors by tier, scored across the dimensions, with this practice's honest
self-assessment row included.

White space
Where the field is genuinely open, and what holding it would require.

Recommended moves
Two or three. Each with the cost and the first step.

Sources
Numbered and dated. Which claims are sourced and which are judgment.

Gaps
What could not be verified. Always populated.
```

## Rules

- The self-assessment row is honest or the exercise is worthless. If the
  practice is behind on a dimension, say so.
- Competitor marketing is an assertion. Verify against what they actually
  deliver before it enters the matrix.
- Never disparage a named competitor in anything client-facing. Internal
  analysis can be blunt. Client-facing material describes what this practice
  does, not what others do badly.
- No performance comparisons. Ever.
- Never use information about a competitor learned from a client or a prospect
  in a way that identifies them.
- For advisor acquisition work, nothing produced here induces a breach of a
  competitor's employment or non-solicitation obligations. Flag anything close
  to that line rather than working around it.
- Client-facing output from this skill goes to RBC DS compliance review.
- Every score has a stated basis. An unexplained score is an opinion in a table.
- Short sentences. Active voice. No emojis. No em dashes.
